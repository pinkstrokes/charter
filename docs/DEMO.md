# Demo: two minutes at the table, then the judges' own scenario

The presenter is the controller, Dana, on a copy of the finished runs that resets in a second or two between tables: company B on its real invoices, company A on the Friday of a payment run nobody has attacked yet. A step bar at the bottom of the page opens each beat; a judge's own scenario goes in on the World or Security screen.

```bash
tools/demo/demo.sh up        # demo server :3100, the evolver watching A and B, the UI
open http://127.0.0.1:5191   # step 1 is company B's moment
tools/demo/demo.sh reset     # between tables (or Reset on the step bar)
```

Once, after the final runs: stop the main server, `tools/demo/demo.sh clone`, start the main server again, `tools/demo/demo.sh build`. The demo server is a separate SpacetimeDB on port 3100; its snapshot and the evolvers' working copy of `data/` live in `~/Developer/charter-demo-stdb`, so nothing done at the table reaches the runs or the repository. The build reads the preset bank-change email once and carries both readings into company A's model cache, so step 2 answers at once; a judge's own email is read live.

## The judges' turn

- **Make something up.** World: a payment (any payer, any invoices, any reference, from any account) or a bill (any vendor, any number, with or without an order). It goes in through the same door as everything the simulation delivered; follow it to the seat's decision and its Why, or to the question it asked. If it asks: "It has seen too few cases like this to act alone, so it asks, and offers the customer's own invoices first; this is exactly when it should ask."
- **Correct anything.** Open any decision's Why panel, *This was wrong*, one line of why. The evolver is watching: it proposes a new version with its replay proof, or says why one answer is not enough evidence. Activate it as the controller; it starts on probation.
- **Attack it.** Security: any email from any address — look-alike domains, prompt injection in a remittance, a refund to a new account, someone claiming to be the CEO. Two readers, the sender check and the kernel decide; every refusal is on the record.
- **Ask why.** Every row opens a Why panel: who, what it asked for, the policies the kernel checked, the facts it saw, the evidence it used, and the row's place in the hash chain.

## Questions to expect

- **Where is the AI?** Reading email (two model families, no tools; an email either reader did not answer waits for a person), writing candidate skill versions from people's corrections, and re-reading a sample of emails for the internal auditor. Every decision is a rule program the kernel runs deterministically; a model-written version goes live only after a replay over every past decision and a person's approval.
- **Does knowledge transfer?** For three months, clearly: company C started from company B's skill and made 2 mistakes instead of 20, and asked people 34 times in seven months instead of 72. After that both ledgers were wrong at about the same rate (15 of 832 against 11 of 816), and we can name why: B's catch-all, "no set fits: apply by contract order", suited B's payers but not C's money sent on account, and C's gate would not hand those cases back to people on two labeled mistakes when it needs three. Where the work differs, the target refuses: B's skill offered to company A failed A's own gate.
- **Your learned bank desk matches less than six hand-written rules.** It does: 783 fewer lines matched alone, 19 fewer wrong, about 41 lines a person matches per mistake avoided, and the hand-written rules fall under the floor in April (99.79%) while the learned desk clears it every month. A wrong match hides a real difference until someone finds it; Charter prices one mistake at 1/tolerable questions (500 at 0.2%) at every desk.
- **Is 3/4 on under-reporting robust?** It is one run. In earlier local runs the same desk ended at 3 and 1 of the 4 test weeks, and at 2 in a trial run tonight on code we then fixed; this run's version also caught store 31's milder under-reporting in June, which earlier runs missed. Collections and payables ended at 3/3 and 2/2 every time.
- **Isn't 0/36 on attacks by construction?** Mostly, yes: the models hold no tools, so the 36 show that no reading of an email gets past the money rules. Not yet tried: forged remittances that misapply real money, poisoning the learning with bad corrections, floods of questions, and attackers from other model families.
- **How sure is "95%"?** The tolerances and minimum counts are engineering values. Taking work away from people (reduced inspection) uses a bound valid however often it is checked; leaving tightened inspection needs a clean run a rule twice as bad would pass by luck less than one time in 20; the suspension test fires early more often than 5%, in the direction of asking more; and the bounds treat checks as independent, which real errors are not.
- **Why not earn autonomy situation by situation live, if the replay halves the mistakes?** Because it is a trade, and it would have taken the moment away: gated that way the seat asks about every new situation, the 28 December payment included, so B makes 7 mistakes alone instead of 14 for 21 more questions. Gating only what a model added catches none of them; they come from behaviours the hand-written skill shares. It is the controller's call, and the next thing to build.
- **Is the competence map a guarantee?** No: it is a coverage certificate for the cash skill, showing where a version would act with no labeled case behind it. The proof is the team check, C16–C18 over every situation and both collections versions.
- **Can seats each be right and still be wrong together?** Yes, and a solver found it before any run did on company A. Cash application rightly leaves a payment it cannot place unapplied and asks; collections rightly chases what is past due; together they chased customers whose own money was waiting to be applied, 72 times on company B's books and 34 on C's. The kernel now computes that fact and the constitution forbids the notice and the fee (C16–C18). The obvious policy, no chase while any cash is unapplied, would also have held a chase a person confirmed was right. The kernel refused once per customer (7 in B, 8 in C), and collections then held on its own, citing the refusal.
- **Why not apply the customer's credit instead of holding?** In company B the controller chose to keep some duplicate payments as credit, and C16 holds the chase while that credit covers what is past due; no seat yet proposes applying it. That is the next step.
- **What if the cash is not identified?** Money identified to no customer protects no one: that customer can still be chased until a person says whose money it is. Money credited to the wrong customer holds back that customer's chase instead.
- **Do late fees follow the letter?** No, the contract: one fee per invoice past its grace period whenever collections decides to chase, whether the notice goes out or not; C17 and C18 refuse fees on their own facts.
- **Who guards the guards?** The kernel binds every seat and person who acts through Charter; the chain and the git anchors show any rewrite of the record. The database's operator could publish other code or write rows directly, so each certificate hashes the rules it enforced and each anchor records the program the database was running.
- **What does a person still do?** Answer questions, answer spot checks, release money, endorse bank changes, approve skill versions and policies, close the books — and get pushed back on when they are wrong. In company B, after its first two months: about four spot checks and half a question a business day, a version to approve every two or three months, one close a month.
- **What did you build this weekend?** From 22:00 on Friday: a SpacetimeDB spike (model calls inside procedures do not block the ledger), then the kernel, seats and governance; company B's real invoices, the evolver and its replay gate, and the red team before 23:30; earned autonomy, internal audit, payables and the web UI overnight; the long tail of payments, the gate's statistics after an adversarial review, company C and the public page on Saturday; the solver's team check and the C16 fix in the afternoon; this evening the fixes a reviewer's critique asked for. 140-odd commits, all in the repository's history.
- **Is the landing page live?** The landing page replays a port of the seats' code in the browser over the runs' recorded data; the live system is the app page, which watches the companies on SpacetimeDB maincloud.
