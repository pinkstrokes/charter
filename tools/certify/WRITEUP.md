# Paste-ready text (for README and Devpost; the main session decides where)

## One line
A solver maps where Charter's cash skill acts with no person's check behind it, and proves across seats that collections cannot chase a customer with the customer's own money, a failure six months of history never showed.

## Paragraph
**Where the cash skill has been checked, and one proof.** A replay shows how a skill did on the payments it has seen. The coverage map shows where it acts with nothing checked behind it.
- The cash skill's versions tell payments apart by 8 conditions. A solver lists every combination of them that can occur under consistency axioms every recorded payment satisfies: 40 situations. Each is built as a concrete payment, a few edits of a real ledger, and read by the seat's own code.
- This is a coverage map for the cash skill, not a proof that it decides rightly. Company B's current version would act alone in 11 situations that none of its 1,225 payments ever fell into, and in 17 that no person ever labeled. B's and C's current versions decide alike in every situation neither company has seen.
- In a replay of B's six months, not a live run, earning autonomy situation by situation instead of rule by rule would have cut mistakes made alone from 14 to 7, for 21 more questions.
- The proof is the team check, across seats and the kernel. It found a team failure: a franchisee pays everything it has past due with a vague reference, cash application rightly asks a person, and collections sends a past-due notice and $400 of late fees the same day (a morning built on company A's real ledger). No seat is wrong on its own, and company A's run never produced it.
- The fix is a constitution amendment written by people, C16 to C18, in force from day one in every company. The solver proves it on all four properties for every situation and both versions of the collections skill. It refutes the obvious policies: they let the fees through, or hold chases a person confirmed were right. In the final runs it held on the real books: collections went to chase 7 of company B's customers and 8 of C's with their own unapplied money, the kernel refused every notice, and none went out. (Runs before the fix, since replaced, had sent 72 such notices in B and 34 in C.)

## Proof beat (one line, spoken, about 12 seconds)
Across seats, it found that a franchisee who pays everything past due could still get a past-due notice and $400 of fees. People wrote C16 to C18; the solver proves they close it in every situation, and in the final run they stopped it for seven of company B's customers.

Shorter, if the beat is tight: "Across seats, it caught collections about to chase seven customers with their own money, and proves the fix people wrote."
